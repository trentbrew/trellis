# About This Work

A little context about what I'm building, why I'm building it, and where I'm coming from. For the technical narrative (reasoning/state gap, decision traces), see **[The Story](./THE-STORY.md)** ([web](https://trellis.computer/architecture/story)).

## What this is

**Trellis** is a local-first, event-sourced graph engine for code, agents, and decisions: durable state you own, with servers optional. They may relay, accelerate, or back up, but never own your data.

Broader than any one package name, this repo is an exploration of what personal computing might look like if it were designed from the ground up for a world of intelligent, autonomous agents, without sacrificing personal sovereignty, ownership, or human agency.

It is an attempt to connect ideas from operating systems, distributed systems, knowledge representation, artificial intelligence, and human-computer interaction into something tangible.

## What this isn't

This isn't a finished product, a definitive architecture, or an attempt to establish a new standard. Many of the ideas here are experimental. Some are hypotheses. Others will almost certainly turn out to be wrong.

I'm not particularly interested in defending ideas simply because I've invested time in them.

## Why I'm building it

I believe there's something worth exploring here. The primary drive isn't "must become a business," though the ecosystem does include open engine code and separate hosted or vertical products; see **[What's Open, What's Sold](./OPEN-SOURCE-STRATEGY.md)** for that line.

I'm building Trellis because I want to use it, because I want to understand what it would take to make it real, and because I suspect the implications extend well beyond the software itself.

## Who this is for

This project will probably resonate most with people interested in the intersection of computing disciplines, particularly those who enjoy questioning foundational assumptions and exploring alternatives to established approaches.

I'm especially interested in connecting with people who know considerably more than I do about the individual disciplines this project touches.

If you see something fundamentally wrong with an idea, an implementation, or an assumption, I'd genuinely like to hear about it. Ideally, I'd like to understand not just *that* something is wrong, but *why*, and what a better way of thinking about it might look like.

## A little about me

I'm an independent software engineer and product designer. I work across disciplines, spend an unreasonable amount of time thinking about systems, and have a habit of taking on work that spans more fields than any one résumé line would suggest.

I'm not a researcher, an operating systems veteran, or an authority on most of the subjects this project touches. I'm one person trying to connect a lot of dots, learn from people who know more than I do, and see how far I can take an idea in public.

That's partly why this work is open.

If any of this interests you, or if you think I'm approaching something the wrong way, I'd love to hear from you.
